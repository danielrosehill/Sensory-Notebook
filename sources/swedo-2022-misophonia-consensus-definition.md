# Swedo SE, et al

An expert panel convened by the Misophonia Research Fund ran a modified Delphi process (4 rounds, at least 80% agreement) to produce the reference definition of misophonia. The definition calls it a disorder of decreased tolerance to specific sounds, driven by pattern or meaning rather than loudness, with triggers often repetitive and mainly from other people's bodies (oral and nasal sounds are the most common). The commonest reactions are anger, irritation, disgust and anxiety, with autonomic arousal; onset is typically in childhood or early adolescence. No consensus was reached on mechanism, prevalence, course, psychiatric vs medical classification, or the relationship to hearing disorders. Full text read for the definition section on 2026-10-14.
