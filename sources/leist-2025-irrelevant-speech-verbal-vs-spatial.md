# Irrelevant speech vs non-speech, verbal vs spatial serial recall

The cleanest available demonstration that the irrelevant sound effect is **task-shaped**,
not just sound-shaped. This is the source that makes the state-dependence in
[`context/profile.md`](../context/profile.md) unsurprising rather than anomalous.

## Design

137 children (mean age ≈ 9;0) and 98 adults. Each did **either** a verbal serial recall
task (pictures of monosyllabic German nouns) **or** a spatial one (dot locations), under
three sound conditions:

| Condition | Material |
|---|---|
| Silence | — |
| Non-speech | Environmental sounds — dog barking, bells, siren |
| Speech | Unfamiliar narrative speech (a Danish newsreader) |

List lengths: 5 items for children; 8 (verbal) and 7 (spatial) for adults. Sound played
at **≈ 59 dB L<sub>Aeq</sub>** during presentation and retention — ordinary conversational
level, not loud.

## Findings

- **Verbal task:** both age groups disrupted, similarly. Speech hurt more than
  environmental sounds; environmental sounds still hurt more than silence.
  F(1.94, 230) = 29.274, p < .001, η²p = .197. No interaction with age.
- **Spatial task:** adults showed **no disruption at all**. Children were impaired, and
  speech and non-speech impaired them equally. Follow-up attributed this entirely to
  third-graders; fourth-graders were unaffected.
- **No habituation** across trials, in either age group or task.

## Authors' interpretation

Adult spatial recall being immune argues against domain-general accounts (changing-state
seriation, attentional refreshing) and fits **phonological-store interference**. The
children's spatial disruption, and its disappearance by fourth grade, is read as
**attention capture** that wanes as attentional control matures. They conclude both
specific interference and attention capture contribute — a duplex-mechanism view.

## Why this matters here

Two things fall straight out of it:

1. The damage is done to **verbal serial short-term memory** specifically. Tasks that
   are not verbal-serial can be immune in the same acoustic environment. Reading,
   writing, composing an email and holding a mental thread are verbal-serial. Sitting in
   the same room not doing those things is not.
2. It was done at **59 dB** — conversational. Loudness is not the variable.

Authors' own practical note: tasks depending on verbal short-term memory (early reading,
spelling, vocabulary, mental arithmetic) are best done away from background speech or
music.

## Caveats named by the authors

Only 8 trials per condition for children; limited evidence of attention capture from
environmental sounds in adults; participants were 8+, so younger school starters are
untested; age and class level strongly confounded (r(67) = .82).
