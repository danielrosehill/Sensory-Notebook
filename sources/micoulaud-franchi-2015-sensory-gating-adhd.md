# Perceptual abnormalities related to sensory gating deficit in adult ADHD

## Design

Three groups of 70 — adults with ADHD, adults with schizophrenia, and healthy controls —
completed the Sensory Gating Inventory (SGI), a self-report measure of perceptual
disturbance related to failures of sensory filtering. Relationships to clinical symptoms
and the SGI's discriminative performance were also examined.

## Findings

- The ADHD group scored **higher on the SGI than both the healthy controls and the
  schizophrenia group**.
- Total SGI score correlated significantly with the **inattention** factor of the ASRS.
- ROC analysis showed good performance discriminating adult ADHD from healthy controls.

## Authors' conclusion

Perceptual abnormalities are core symptoms of adult ADHD. Their proposed framing:
attention in these patients may be "involuntarily drowned by many irrelevant
environmental stimuli."

## Companion EEG paper (same group, same year)

The *Biological Psychology* paper reports **reduced P50 suppression** in adults with
ADHD relative to healthy subjects — an electrophysiological index of pre-attentive
sensory gating — and reports that SGI scores and P50 suppression were correlated. This
is the bridge from self-report to a physiological measure, and it is what allows the
gating account to be more than a restatement of the complaint.

## Caveats

- Self-report instruments for "filtering" problems and self-report instruments for
  inattention share obvious surface content; a correlation between SGI and ASRS
  inattention is partly expected from item overlap alone.
- P50 suppression is a paired-click paradigm using clicks, not speech. Generalising from
  reduced P50 gating to "cannot ignore a conversation" is an inference, not a finding.
- Cross-sectional, clinic-recruited samples.
