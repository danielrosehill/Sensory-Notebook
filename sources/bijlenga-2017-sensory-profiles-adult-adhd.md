# Atypical sensory profiles as core features of adult ADHD

The standard citation for "sensory hypersensitivity is elevated in adult ADHD, and it is
not just undiagnosed autism."

## Design

116 adults diagnosed with ADHD completed the Adolescent/Adult Sensory Profile (AASP-NL)
and the Autism-spectrum Quotient (AQ). Prevalence of hyper- and hyposensitivity was
compared against AASP normative values. Multivariate binary logistic regression tested
the contribution of autistic symptoms, age, gender, ADHD subtype, self-reported severity,
comorbidity and medication.

## Findings

- Adults with ADHD reported **both more hyposensitivity and more hypersensitivity** than
  norms — the two are not opposite ends of one axis here.
- The differences were most apparent in the **auditory** modality and in activity level.
- They also reported more autism-spectrum symptoms and more subthreshold ASD indication
  than norms — and the sensory findings held after controlling for these.
- Effects described as especially pronounced in females.

## Authors' conclusion

Sensory hypo- and hypersensitivity may be viewed as key features of adult ADHD regardless
of autistic symptoms, and may be worth assessing in the adult ADHD diagnostic procedure.

## Caveats — significant

- **No control group.** Comparisons were made against normative data in the AASP manual.
  This is the methodological objection later authors raise, and it matters: the norm
  sample differs from a clinic sample on far more than ADHD.
- Entirely self-report, in a group already selected for reporting attentional complaints.
- AASP quadrant scores ("low registration," "sensation seeking," "sensory sensitivity,"
  "sensation avoiding") are Dunn-model constructs and inherit that model's contested
  status — see [`spd-diagnostic-status.md`](spd-diagnostic-status.md).

## Surrounding literature (secondary, unverified individually)

- Kooij et al. (2001) — earlier report of auditory hypersensitivity in adult ADHD.
- Treister et al. (2015) — hypersensitivity to pain in adults with ADHD vs norms.
- Panagiotidi et al. — sensory processing sensitivity associated with ADHD traits in the
  general population, supporting a spectrum reading.
- Later work with an actual control group found self-reported ADHD symptoms associated
  with the low registration, sensation seeking, and sensory sensitivity quadrants.
- Some studies indicate methylphenidate normalises some sensory hypersensitivities. This
  is the thread to pull for the open medication question in
  [`context/scope.md`](../context/scope.md) — **not yet verified, do not cite as settled.**
