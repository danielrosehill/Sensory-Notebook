# Arnal LH, Flinker A, Kleinschmidt A, Giraud AL, Poeppel D

Screams carry 'roughness', fast amplitude modulation around 30–150 Hz, which sets them apart from speech (about 4–5 Hz) and from other vocalisations. Rougher sounds, including artificial alarms, were rated more alarming and drove stronger amygdala responses in fMRI (n=20). This establishes screams as aversive to listeners in general by acoustic design, so a strong reaction to them does not need an unusual trait to explain it. The study used adult screams; infant screams were flagged as follow-up work. Abstract and press-level reading.
