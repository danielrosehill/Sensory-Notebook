# Perceptual load counters increased distraction in ADHD

Applies Lavie's load theory of selective attention to ADHD distractibility.

## Load theory in one paragraph

Perceptual capacity is limited and is allocated involuntarily. When the task in front of
you has **high perceptual load**, capacity is exhausted on task-relevant input and
irrelevant distractors are not processed. When load is **low**, spare capacity spills
onto whatever else is present, and selection has to happen later, via cognitive control.
Lavie et al. (2004) added the second half: high **working memory / cognitive control**
load has the *opposite* effect and *increases* distractor interference, because the
control mechanism that suppresses already-perceived distractors is itself loaded.

## Findings

- Adults with ADHD showed **increased distraction** by salient-but-irrelevant distractors
  compared with age- and IQ-matched controls.
- Increasing perceptual load **reduced** distractor interference, and did so **equally
  effectively in both groups**.

## Why the "equally effectively" matters

If the ADHD deficit were in early perceptual selection, raising perceptual load should
have helped the ADHD group *more* (or not at all). That it helped both groups the same
amount points the deficit at the **late / cognitive-control** stage rather than the
early sensory-filtering stage. That is a direct tension with the sensory-gating account
in [`micoulaud-franchi-2015-sensory-gating-adhd.md`](micoulaud-franchi-2015-sensory-gating-adhd.md),
and the tension is unresolved.

## Caveats

- The authors themselves flag that a response-competition task may not be the right index
  of the *irrelevant* distraction that ADHD patients actually complain about.
- The distractors were visual (salient irrelevant images), not speech. Cross-modal
  transfer is assumed, not shown, here.
- The whole load-theory framework has a live challenger — the **dilution account**
  (Tsal & Benoni, 2010), which argues the effect of "load" is really neutral items
  diluting distractor processing.

## Related from the same group

Forster & Lavie (2016), *Psychological Science*, "Establishing the attention-distractibility
trait": in a non-clinical sample, ADHD symptom scores predicted interference from
*irrelevant* distractors specifically, and high perceptual load eliminated distraction
regardless of symptom score. Supports a continuum rather than a categorical account.
