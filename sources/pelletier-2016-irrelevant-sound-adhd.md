# Vulnerability to the Irrelevant Sound Effect in Adult ADHD

The most directly on-point study for the question in
[`answers/260812-background-speech-and-focus.md`](../answers/260812-background-speech-and-focus.md).

## Design

32 adults with a diagnosis of ADHD and 32 controls performed a serial recall task under
two conditions: quiet, and with task-irrelevant background sound they were instructed to
ignore. Described by the authors as an ecologically valid adaptation of the irrelevant
sound paradigm.

## Findings

- The ADHD group recalled less accurately than controls **in both conditions** — so part
  of the gap is baseline short-term memory, not distraction.
- The **additional** decrement caused by irrelevant sound was larger in the ADHD group.
  This is the elevated-ISE result.
- Performance under irrelevant sound correlated positively with self-reported attention
  problems on a clinical symptom scale.
- Sustained attention appeared preserved: the impact of the irrelevant sound **diminished
  over the course of the task**.

## Authors' conclusion

Adults with ADHD show impaired short-term memory and low resistance to distraction, with
sustained attention intact.

## What this source does and does not support

Supports: the interference is larger in ADHD, and its size tracks self-rated inattention.

Does **not** support: any claim about mechanism. The design cannot separate
interference-by-process from attentional capture, and cannot say whether the effect is
sensory or attentional.

Note the habituation finding runs against a naive "ADHD never habituates" story, and
is worth holding onto — it is a point where lived experience and the lab may diverge.
