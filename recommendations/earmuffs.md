# Earmuffs, worn over earplugs

Personal experience, not evidence. Not medical advice — see the disclaimer in the
[README](../README.md).

## The problem

The top of the escalation ladder: situations where a closed door is not enough and earplugs
alone are not enough either. In practice this has been someone else's television while I am
trying to study or work.

## What was tried

Earmuffs worn **on top of** earplugs, rather than instead of them — the doubling-up
approach.

## What happened

Tried sometimes, never adopted. **I have not found them as comfortable**, and earplugs
remain my general go-to.

## What it did not fix

- **Comfort.** Same failure mode that decided every other entry in this directory: the
  question is not what a thing blocks, it is whether you will keep it on.
- **No attenuation claim is being made either way.** Doubling up plausibly blocks more than
  plugs alone; I have not measured it and neither has this notebook. The verdict here is
  about wearability only. <!-- FLAG: do not let a later edit turn "less comfortable" into
  "less effective". -->
- **Nothing is recorded about the specific muffs.** A different pair might sit differently.
  This is one person's experience of an unrecorded model, not a verdict on the category.

## Transferability

- **Worth trying if plugs alone are not covering it**, since muffs-over-plugs is the
  standard way to stack protection and it costs nothing to test if you already own plugs.
- **Expect the comfort tax to be the deciding factor**, as it was here. Clamping force and
  heat are the usual complaints, and they compound with an earplug already in place.
- **If you get on with them, you have an option this notebook does not.** The absence of a
  positive verdict here is one person's ears, not a finding.
