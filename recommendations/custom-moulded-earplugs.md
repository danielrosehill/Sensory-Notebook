# Custom moulded earplugs, made by an audiologist

Personal experience, not evidence. Nothing on this page is a research claim, and the
attenuation questions it raises are deliberately left open — see
[`questions/260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md`](../questions/260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md).

Not medical advice. See the disclaimer in the [README](../README.md).

## The problem

We have lived in Jerusalem for ten years, and **every apartment has been noisy in a
different way**. There is a pattern to how this happens: you view a place, it is pretty
quiet, you move in — and it turns out you viewed it at a quiet time of day. That has
happened to us recurrently.

In the previous apartment what emerged after moving in was **honking, at night**. We could
hear it clearly in the bedroom, and I could hear it while working.

The thing that made this different from the background-speech problem is that **it was not
about concentration**. It was the feeling of not having control of your environment. The
place that is supposed to be your safe space from the noise of the city is suddenly
invaded by the noise of the city. I would not go so far as to say it drove me to insanity,
but it was extremely stressful, and it got me to the point of looking for literally any
solution under the sun.

**Why it took so long to buy anything.** This is something I had struggled with for many
years, and it was never a priority. It never felt worth a hundred dollars. What changed
was not new information — it was that my daily happiness was at stake. That is worth
naming, because I suspect the same arithmetic keeps a lot of people from buying the thing
that would help them.

In roughly this order, during the same desperate search:

1. **Over-ear ANC headphones — Sony**, model not recorded and not considered important.

3. **Loop earplugs.** Worked, but superseded on comfort — separate entry:

4. **Custom moulded plugs from an audiologist**, taken from impressions of my own ears.
   Two pairs, deliberately in different materials:
   - **Silicone — soft.** For sleeping.
   - **Acrylic — hard.** For daytime use.

The two-material split was the point. A soft plug is what you can lie on; a hard plug is
what you want when you are upright and moving around.

## What I actually asked for

Worth recording separately, because the brief explains the result. I described the
specific problems rather than asking for a product: something to attenuate noise, for
sleeping, and for the honking. Then one further requirement — that I wanted something I
could also wear **during the day for extended periods, potentially the whole time I was
working**, at **roughly the same attenuation**.

So the two pairs were specified from the outset as *equivalent in attenuation, different
in wearing posture*. That is the missing piece behind the later advice to reuse the sleep
pair for parenting: they were never meant to be two different strengths.

## What they physically are

Determined by looking at the plugs on 2026-08-12, not from documentation:

- Soft, lightweight, **skin-toned**, with a full concha shell and a small removal tab.
- Marked **red on one and blue on the other** — the standard audiology right/left
  convention.
- **No filter bore is visible on either.** That points to **solid, unfiltered** plugs,
  which per [`../sources/custom-earplug-attenuation-and-filters.md`](../sources/custom-earplug-attenuation-and-filters.md)
  sits in the **~25–30 dB** region rather than at a filtered 9, 15 or 25 dB value.

The observation is consistent with the brief above: a solid plug is what you get when you
ask for one level of attenuation for everything, rather than a filter tuned to preserve
speech or music.

## What happened

**They work, and they are one of the most useful things I have ever bought.** These are
the ones I wear all the time, in preference to everything else on that list.

**The comfort is the whole thing, and it is not a minor point.** Earplugs can become their
own sensory problem — generic ones feel dry and uncomfortable sitting in your ear, so the
cure introduces a new irritation. The customs are **by far the most inert** of anything I
have tried. I am aware they are in my ears, but they do not nag at me. (I keep them washed,
as you would with any earplug, but that is hygiene, not comfort.)

**The appointment itself was a pivotal moment, separately from the product.** Being
somewhere where the request was completely ordinary — the audiologists' reaction was along
the lines of *this is something we see all the time, it's a perfect solution* — was
**incredibly validating** in itself. That is not a feature of the earplugs. It is a feature
of walking into a place where the thing you have been quietly working around for years is a
routine Tuesday.

**The parenting follow-up.** I became a father in July 2025. A screaming baby was really
challenging, and I went back looking for a pair that would take the edge off without being
unsafe. The audiologist's answer was that **the silicone sleep pair I already owned was
what I should use** — and declined to sell me another pair even though I would have bought
one. I appreciated that a lot.

**They kept earning it after the original problem went away.** In the current apartment
there is drilling and very loud construction work going on. We have not finished unboxing
and I am already very glad these are in the cabinet. A thing bought for one apartment's
noise has turned out to be a thing you own for the next one's.

## What it did not fix

- **It did not settle whether sleep-grade attenuation is the right attenuation for
  parenting.** The advice I was given was to reuse the sleep pair, and I have no reason to
  doubt it — but "the pair you already own" and "the pair with the right curve for this
  situation" are two different claims, and only the first one was actually tested. Filed as
  an open question rather than assumed:
  [`questions/260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md`](../questions/260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md).
- **It did not solve the red-alert siren problem.** Living in Israel, there is a hard
  constraint on any hearing protection worn at home: during wartime, the last place you
  want to be is somewhere you cannot hear an emergency alert. Custom plugs do not come with
  an answer to that, and I do not have one. It is a geographically specific problem, but for
  anyone here it is the binding one.
- **They are a mitigation, not control of your environment.** They made the honking
  survivable. They did not give me back the thing that had actually been taken, which was a
  bedroom that was quiet because it was quiet.
- **Whether they help with the primary auditory trigger is not recorded.** The profile's
  main problem is intelligible background speech while concentrating, which is a filtering
  problem rather than a loudness problem. Whether these plugs do anything for it, in an
  open-plan office or otherwise, is simply not something this file knows.

## Transferability

For someone else to get the same result:

- **You need an audiologist who takes ear impressions and makes custom moulds.** This is not
  a mail-order product; the fit is the entire value.
- **The gain is comfort, not attenuation.** If off-the-shelf plugs already sit comfortably
  in your ears and you wear them, custom moulds buy you much less. The before/after here is
  "unwearable" to "wearable, all day, by choice" — not "quiet" to "quieter". If you have
  ever abandoned earplugs because they felt dry and intrusive rather than because they
  didn't block enough, this is the specific thing custom moulds fix.
- **Ask for material by use case, not one pair for everything.** Soft silicone for sleeping
  and lying on your side; hard acrylic for daytime. Buying one pair to cover both is the
  mistake this avoided.
- **Buy spares if you can afford to.** Ideally I would have a spare of each material. In
  practice I have one spare acrylic pair. These are small objects that live in a cabinet
  and get carried around; losing the only one at the wrong moment is the obvious failure
  mode.
- **Cost was ~USD 100 for the set of two**, and will vary a lot by country and provider.
  Treat it as an order of magnitude. Worth knowing that the price is what deferred this for
  years, and in hindsight the deferral was the expensive part.
- **Judge the provider by whether they will talk you out of a purchase.** The most useful
  thing mine did was tell me I did not need to buy anything else. A provider who sells you a
  third pair for every new situation is telling you something.
