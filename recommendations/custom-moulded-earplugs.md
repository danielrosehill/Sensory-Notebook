# Custom moulded earplugs, made by an audiologist

Personal experience, not evidence. Nothing on this page is a research claim, and the
attenuation questions it raises are deliberately left open — see
[`questions/260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md`](../questions/260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md).

Not medical advice. See the disclaimer in the [README](../README.md).

## The problem

Off-the-shelf earplugs — the generic ones you buy in a shop — were **super
uncomfortable**. Uncomfortable enough to be the deciding factor, not a minor gripe. An
earplug you cannot stand wearing does not attenuate anything, because it is not in your
ear.

## What was tried

1. **Generic off-the-shelf earplugs.** Rejected on comfort.
2. **Custom moulded plugs from an audiologist**, taken from impressions of my own ears.
   Two pairs, deliberately in different materials:
   - **Silicone — soft.** For sleeping.
   - **Acrylic — hard.** For daytime use.

The two-material split was the point. A soft plug is what you can lie on; a hard plug is
what you want when you are upright and moving around.

## What happened

**They work, and they are one of the most useful things I have ever bought.** Comfortable
enough to actually wear, which is the whole difference from the shop-bought ones.

**The appointment itself was a pivotal moment, separately from the product.** Being
somewhere where the request was completely ordinary — the audiologists' reaction was
along the lines of *this is something we see all the time, it's a perfect solution* — was
**incredibly validating** in itself. That is not a feature of the earplugs. It is a
feature of walking into a place where the thing you have been quietly working around for
years is a routine Tuesday.

**The parenting follow-up.** I became a father in July 2025. A screaming baby was really
challenging, and I went back looking for a pair that would take the edge off without
being unsafe. The audiologist's answer was that **the silicone sleep pair I already owned
was what I should use** — and declined to sell me another pair even though I would have
bought one. I appreciated that a lot.

## What it did not fix

- **It did not settle whether sleep-grade attenuation is the right attenuation for
  parenting.** The advice I was given was to reuse the sleep pair, and I have no reason to
  doubt it — but "the pair you already own" and "the pair with the right curve for this
  situation" are two different claims, and only the first one was actually tested. Filed
  as an open question rather than assumed:
  [`questions/260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md`](../questions/260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md).
- **It did not solve the red-alert siren problem.** Living in Israel, there is a hard
  constraint on any hearing protection worn at home: during wartime, the last place you
  want to be is somewhere you cannot hear an emergency alert. Custom plugs do not come
  with an answer to that, and I do not have one. It is a geographically specific problem,
  but for anyone here it is the binding one.
- **Whether they help with the primary auditory trigger is not recorded.** The profile's
  main problem is intelligible background speech while concentrating, which is a
  filtering problem rather than a loudness problem. Whether these plugs do anything for
  it, in an open-plan office or otherwise, is simply not something this file knows.

## Transferability

For someone else to get the same result:

- **You need an audiologist who takes ear impressions and makes custom moulds.** This is
  not a mail-order product; the fit is the entire value.
- **The gain is comfort, not attenuation.** If off-the-shelf plugs already sit
  comfortably in your ears and you wear them, custom moulds buy you much less. The
  before/after here is "unwearable" to "wearable", not "quiet" to "quieter".
- **Ask for material by use case, not one pair for everything.** Soft silicone for
  sleeping and lying on your side; hard acrylic for daytime. Buying one pair to cover
  both is the mistake this avoided.
- **Cost is in the low hundreds of dollars**, and will vary a lot by country and
  provider. Treat the figure in the front-matter as an order of magnitude.
- **Judge the provider by whether they will talk you out of a purchase.** The most useful
  thing mine did was tell me I did not need to buy anything else. A provider who sells
  you a third pair for every new situation is telling you something.
