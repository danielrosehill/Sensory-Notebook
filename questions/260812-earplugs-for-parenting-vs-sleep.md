# Is the right earplug attenuation for a screaming baby the same as for sleep?

I have two pairs of custom moulded earplugs from an audiologist — a soft silicone pair
for sleeping and a hard acrylic pair for daytime. Background in
[`recommendations/custom-moulded-earplugs.md`](../recommendations/custom-moulded-earplugs.md).

I became a father in July 2025, and the screaming-baby end of it was really challenging. I
went back to the audiologist looking for a pair that would take the edge off without being
unsafe — meaning, without cutting me off from the baby. What I was told was that the
silicone sleep pair I already have is what I should use, and they would not sell me another
pair even though I would have bought one. I thought that was a good answer and I have no
reason to doubt it.

But I do not want to assume it is correct just because it was generous. So:

1. **Is the attenuation you want for sleep actually the same as the attenuation you want
   for parenting?** These feel like different requirements to me. For sleep the goal is to
   hear as little as possible. For parenting the goal is to still hear the baby clearly —
   just not at full intensity. Those could easily want different amounts, or different
   *shapes* of attenuation across frequency, rather than the same plug in both cases.

2. **What is actually unsafe here, and what is just uncomfortable?** I framed the request
   as "take the edge off without being unsafe", but I do not know where the real line is —
   for the baby's sake or for my hearing's.

3. **The red-alert siren constraint.** Living in Israel, this is the one that actually
   worries me. During wartime the last place you want to be is somewhere you cannot hear
   an emergency alert. What level of attenuation still leaves a red-alert siren reliably
   audible, including asleep? This is a geographically specific problem and I am aware
   most readers will not have it, but for anyone here it decides the whole question.

## Status

**Answered 2026-08-12** — [`answers/260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md`](../answers/260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md).

Points 1 and 2 are answered: the advice holds, and sleep is the harder case of the two, so
reusing the sleep pair errs in the safe direction. **Point 3 is not answered numerically** —
the Israeli siren specification is not publicly published, and the answer says so instead
of substituting a plausible figure.
