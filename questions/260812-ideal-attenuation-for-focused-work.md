# What is the ideal level of attenuation for studying or working — and does it change if you are more sensory-overloaded than I am?

The companion question to
[`260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md`](260812-earplugs-for-parenting-vs-sleep.md).
That one is about safety limits — how much you can block and still hear a baby or a siren.
This one is about the opposite end: what you should actually be aiming for when the goal is
concentration.

1. **Is there an ideal attenuation level for focused work, and is it a maximum?** My
   instinct is to assume more blocking is better, but I do not know that. The profile's
   primary trigger is intelligible background speech
   ([`../context/profile.md`](../context/profile.md)), and if the problem is
   intelligibility rather than volume, then the target might be "drop speech below the
   point where it resolves into words" rather than "make it as quiet as possible". Those
   are different specs and they might want different products.

2. **Does the answer differ by severity?** I am interested in what this looks like for
   someone *more* sensory-overloaded than I am, not just for me. Whether the right level
   scales with how badly affected you are, or whether there is a single acoustic target
   everyone is aiming at, seems like a real question rather than an obvious one.

3. **Is there a point where blocking too much becomes its own problem?** Silence,
   occlusion effect, hearing your own pulse and chewing — anecdotally these bother some
   people. Where that sits relative to the useful range is not something I know.

## Why this is not obvious from the products

Every earplug is sold on a single NRR or SNR number, which is one figure for a thing that
varies by frequency. The [existing recommendations](../recommendations/) are all decided on
comfort, because comfort is what determined whether the plug got worn — none of them
involved comparing attenuation curves. So the notebook currently has strong opinions about
wearability and no data at all about levels.
