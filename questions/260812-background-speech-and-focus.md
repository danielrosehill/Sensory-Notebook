# Why does background conversation only wreck my focus when I'm trying to focus?

I have ADHD, and I've always had a specific kind of sensitivity to sound that can be
roughly described like this: when I'm working, trying to focus on something, I find that
background sound — background conversations in particular — really puts my focus across.
Open-office designs are inherently very challenging for me for that reason.

I don't know if this is a specific pathology, or a recognised part of the overall ADHD
picture.

I've had hearing testing done and organically my hearing is totally normal.

And strangely, it's very focused, very specific for me — by which I mean, if I'm relaxing
in a space I can hear people having phone calls and conversations and it doesn't bother
me at all. It's only when I'm trying to focus that it's hard.

I've asked other people, including my wife, about whether this bothers her at all. She
can actually tune *in* and hear somebody speak, whereas I can't tune it *out*. I think
that's a subtle but vastly telling difference in how maybe neurotypical and neurodiverse
people can experience the exact same sensory input from a totally different perspective.

So: how might this be defined? Is it pretty typical of ADHD, and what specific mechanism
drives it?
